Understanding Computer, Internet, and Tooling Basics

A computer is an electronic device that manipulates information or data. It has the ability to store, retrieve, and process data. You can use a computer to type documents, send emails, play games, browse the web, and much more.

What are the basic parts of a computer?

  • Motherboard: The motherboard is the main circuit board that holds the computer’s essential components, such as memory, connectors, and expansion slots. It serves as the central hub that connects and allows communication between all parts of the computer.
  • Central Processing Unit (CPU): The CPU (Central Processing Unit) is often referred to as the brain of the computer. It executes instructions, performs calculations, and processes data. The CPU is a small chip inserted into the motherboard’s CPU socket. Processor speed is measured in hertz (Hz), typically in gigahertz (GHz — billions of cycles per second) or megahertz (MHz — millions of cycles per second).
  • Memory (RAM): RAM (Random Access Memory) is a temporary storage area that the CPU uses to quickly access data and instructions. The more RAM a computer has, the faster it can run and the more programs it can handle simultaneously. However, RAM is volatile memory—its contents are lost when the computer is turned off, so saving work regularly is essential.
  • Storage: Permanent storage for files and programs is provided by devices like Hard Disk Drives (HDDs) or Solid State Drives (SSDs). HDDs store data on spinning platters, while SSDs use flash memory with no moving parts. SSDs are faster and more reliable but generally more expensive and offer less storage space than HDDs.
  • Power Supply Unit (PSU): The PSU converts electrical power from an outlet into a usable form for the computer. It distributes power to the motherboard, CPU, storage devices, and other components.
  • Expansion Cards: Expansion cards add extra functionality to a computer and are inserted into the motherboard. Common examples include:
    • Video Card (GPU): Renders and displays graphics on the screen.
    • Sound Card: Processes audio data and sends it to speakers or headphones.
    • Network Card: Enables the computer to connect to networks and the internet.
  • Input Devices: Devices like keyboards and mice that allow users to enter data and control the computer.
  • Output Devices: Devices that present data from the computer to the user, such as monitors, printers, and speakers.

How can you effectively work with your keyboard, mouse and other pointing devices?

To effectively work with your keyboard, mouse, and other pointing devices:

  • prolonged misuse of these devices can lead to serious health problems down the road.
  • Mouse:The first tip is to be mindful of the mouse grip. When working on serious tasks for work or during intense gaming moments, you may tend to grip the mouse too hard. However, this can lead to serious hand and wrist issues. To prevent this, you want to make sure to hold the mouse gently and keep your fingers nice and relaxed. Another tip is to keep your mouse the same height as your keyboard. You don't want to have to reach up or down to use the mouse, as this can strain your wrist and arm muscles over time.
  • Ergonomic keyboard and mouse: The second tip is to consider using an ergonomic keyboard and mouse. These tools are designed to reduce wrist strain by keeping your hands in a more natural position. While prices for these devices can vary and may be a costly purchase for your current budget, they can be a good investment in your overall long-term health. So, you might consider starting to research an ergonomic keyboard and mouse to use.
  • PostureYou also want to be mindful of your overall posture when sitting at the computer for hours on end. Being slouched over your computer and putting your wrist in an unnatural position at the keyboard will lead to long-term health issues. So, you want to make sure to sit up straight when working at your computer and maintain a healthy and natural wrist position.
  • Keyboard Shortcuts: The last tip would be to use keyboard shortcuts whenever possible to help minimize typing. There are dozens of shortcuts available for tasks such as navigating the web, navigating around your operating system, working within a code editor, and more. So, it’s best to research some of these keyboard shortcuts and and start incorporating them into your daily computer usage. Learn and use keyboard shortcuts to increase efficiency.
  • Keep your mouse and keyboard clean for optimal performance.
  • Adjust your workspace to be comfortable and ergonomic.

What are the different types of internet service providers?

  • Internet service provider (ISP) It’s a company that sells access to the global internet network, essentially. And they come in three different tiers.
  • First tier: The first tier are the giant conglomerate companies, which have the infrastructure to handle most (if not all) of their network traffic independently.
  • Second tier: The second tier are the country-wide providers, which sometimes rent access to tier 1 networks but can stand fairly well on their own.
  • Third tier: The third tier are the small companies that primarily focus on providing internet to local markets, and rely on larger ISPs to provide their infrastructure.
  • Fibre Optic:Fibre optic connections rely on glass or plastic fibres to transmit data via light, resulting in very high connection speeds and data exchange.
  • Cable Connections:Cable connections use the same infrastructure as a cable television provider, which often makes them readily available in many regions.
  • DSL:DSL connections use the infrastructure that landline phone services use. Because of the prevalence of this infrastructure, DSL is available in areas where cable might not be. But it is also frequently a slower option.
  • Dial-up:uses the phone lines, but requires exclusive connection (disabling the use of the line for phone purposes when connected to the internet). This is a much older technology that has fallen into disuse.
  • Satellite connections:Satellite connections use an array of satellites orbiting the earth to connect various devices across the world. And finally, similar to that, a 5G home internet provider uses the cell tower infrastructure to keep you online.
